How to read the drawings

Forest is mortgage borrowing or its payment, explicitly labelled.

Coral follows your money into a deposit or saving contribution.

Dashed outlines recall a previous state or indicate an uncalculated future.

House body: deposit + mortgage = purchase price. The roof is a recognisable outline, not extra value. Monthly strips and future paths are schematic, never scaled financial results.

01 / The situation

A home you want. Money you have.

Learn: A home has a price; savings are finite.

Transition: The home enters first. The savings envelope opens beside it after a short hold. No debt terminology yet.

Interaction & continuity

Continue, Back and Skip are always available. No financial interaction yet.

Home and envelope arrive separately; hold until Continue.

Home silhouette and original savings become the purchase context.

Price £400,000.00; allocatable savings £100,000.00. Buying costs already set aside outside this illustration.

02 / Choose the deposit

Some of your savings become your deposit.

Learn: The deposit comes out of the same savings you already have.

Transition: A broad coral ribbon leaves the savings envelope, travels left and becomes the house’s deposit band. The envelope visibly empties by the same amount.

Interaction & continuity

Demonstration first. Continue can settle immediately; no compulsory wait.

Transit drawing: ribbon is a route, not additional money. Settle into 03; never hold duplicate monetary totals mid-transfer.

The ribbon resolves into the deposit boundary used by the calculator.

Target deposit £40,000.00. No extra money is created during transfer.

03 / Choose the deposit

You choose how much goes in.

Learn: More deposit means less borrowing AND less money left outside.

Transition: The ribbon settles. Borrowing fills the rest of the rectangular house body. A handle appears exactly on the shared deposit/borrowing boundary.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: drag the boundary; keyboard arrows or exact money entry give the same control. These are drawings, not working controls in R0.

Hold indefinitely. After an optional adjustment, Continue follows this chosen deposit into the next experiment.

One deposit state controls house fill and retained savings; Skip carries it forward.

Deposit £40,000.00; mortgage £360,000.00; retained £60,000.00. Roof is decorative; the rectangular body alone encodes the purchase price.

04 / More deposit

Put more in. Borrow less.

Learn: The extra deposit replaces borrowing pound for pound.

Transition: Keep the house fixed. Lift its deposit boundary; let the old boundary remain as a dashed reference. The coral route visibly drains the envelope.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: Try more makes an explicit comparison branch. Your original choice remains pinned; Back/Skip restores it.

One proposed 700 ms transfer, then hold. Illustrative experiment: £40,000.00 to £80,000.00, not a new default.

Same house, envelope and deposit handle. Comparison is distinct from the user’s selected state.

Example A: £80,000.00 deposit; £320,000.00 mortgage; £20,000.00 retained.

05 / More deposit

Less debt to charge interest on.

Learn: Borrowing less can reduce interest costs with the rate and repayment term held fixed.

Transition: Follow a forest line from the mortgage region out of the house. It becomes an interest-cost trail outside the home. The previous larger trail stays as a dashed outline.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: replay this single cause and effect; no separate exercise.

Reframe the debt edge; reveal the shorter trail only after the borrowing change settles. Hold before introducing pricing.

Debt identity survives. The cost trail can later explain validated interest outputs.

Conceptual comparison over the same repayment term. Trail lengths are schematic, not amounts. Interest is never part of the house price.

06 / More deposit

Sometimes you cross a pricing threshold.

Learn: A better mortgage rate is conditional on reaching a deal band, not on every extra pound.

Transition: The borrowing edge becomes a simple ruler. The marker reaches the fictional 80% borrowing boundary; only then does the attached rate tag turn over.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: scrub across a supplied band boundary; retain a keyboard alternative. Real eligibility must come from validated data.

Two visible moments: marker reaches line, then tag changes. No smoothly decreasing rate animation.

The same borrowing ratio later explains a calculator pricing step.

Fictional 80% band example only; no lender offer or numeric rate. 80% means mortgage as a share of the home price. Actual bands may differ.

07 / More deposit

Now follow the monthly payment.

Learn: Less borrowing, and possibly a lower rate, can reduce the required repayment for the same term.

Transition: The mortgage region leads into a forest payment strip. Keep the original strip’s endpoint as an outline while the current payment retracts.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: optional replay; manual Continue. No auto-advance while someone is exploring.

The house recedes; the payment strip becomes the focal object. Do not add saving labels until its contraction lands.

Payment remains a separate monthly flow, never an opening asset.

Conceptual monthly payment; no quoted repayment, no amount or scaled comparison. Same repayment term assumed.

08 / More deposit

The same monthly budget has room left.

Learn: A lower payment leaves more of a fixed mortgage-and-saving budget available for saving.

Transition: Reveal a fixed outer budget bracket around the payment strip from 07. Its freed space becomes a coral monthly-saving tab. That tab repeats once per month, not on purchase day.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: pause, replay or reverse. Saving/investing the surplus is an explicit comparison assumption.

Keep outer endpoints fixed. Shrink forest, expand coral; then one tab steps into the next month. Hold on the split.

The shared budget and contribution destination can later become inputs; no household affordability claim.

10 / Less deposit

Outside the home, you still have choices.

Learn: Retained money is not automatically invested; cash and investments have different roles.

Transition: The envelope opens into two clearly labelled sleeves. A cash reserve stays in the left sleeve; the optional allocation moves into the right sleeve only by choice.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: choose Keep in cash or Invest some. R0 shows the investment branch only; neither is a recommendation.

Preserve the envelope outline while separating the destinations. Introduce uncertainty beside investments, not in fine print.

Cash and investment destinations retain their meanings in the calculator.

Illustrative B: £20,000.00 cash reserve plus £40,000.00 initially invested. Investments can lose value; cash is an alternative.

11 / Less deposit

A head start, or more added each month?

Learn: A smaller deposit can create a larger initial investment; a larger deposit may enable larger monthly additions.

Transition: Open two labelled lanes sharing one start line. Initial balances are outlined containers. Later additions are small solid tabs at month markers. Show cash reserve separately in both lanes.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: inspect either lane. Neither is selected as a winner. Same monthly funding and surplus destination in both.

Reveal the opening containers first, then monthly tabs. Do not combine them into an unexplained growing total.

Opening allocation and monthly contribution remain separate fields and graphical objects.

A: £80,000.00 deposit, £20,000.00 cash, £0.00 initial investment. B: £40,000.00 deposit, £20,000.00 cash, £40,000.00 initial investment. Monthly tab sizes are conceptual, not scaled financial outputs.

12 / Time & handover

Then time changes the picture.

Learn: Debt is repaid; cash and investments can change through contributions and returns.

Transition: The monthly lane extends into a time rail. Keep three separate tracks: outstanding mortgage, cash and investments. Investment branches extend both upwards and downwards.

Interaction & continuity

PROPOSED: pause the date marker or scrub it. Every view uses the same selected date.

Advance one date marker across the same tracks; outline future states rather than invent balances. No crossover or maximum is drawn.

The time rail becomes the comparison-date control. Opening deposit does not turn into growing equity.

Conceptual, not to scale. No balances, probabilities, forecasts or ranked outcome. Future cash and debt depend on supported assumptions.

House, retained money, deposit boundary and date rail become the familiar instrument.

R0 review handover · Owner decision pending

Three things to scrutinise.

  1. Does the money feel conserved? Frames 02–04 and 09: can you track the same savings into and back out of the house without reading the arithmetic?
  2. Does the monthly consequence make sense? Frames 05–08: do borrowing, interest, conditional pricing and the fixed monthly budget form one chain? In 11, are initial balances unmistakably different from later additions?
  3. Does the ending feel like taking control? Frames 13–14: would these familiar objects feel usable? The detailed calculator composition is still open.

Decision vocabulary: Accept / Revise / Try alternative / Stop. R0 approval concerns the storytelling direction only. Work stops here for visual feedback.

Opening examples: A — £80,000.00 deposit, £320,000.00 mortgage, £20,000.00 cash, £0.00 invested. The 80% borrowing-share marker is a fictional pricing boundary, not a lender rule. No mortgage rates, payments, interest totals, returns, probabilities or optimal deposit are computed. Monthly saving is a conceptual successor-model behaviour; current engine results do not include reinvested payment differences. Investments can lose value.

Research translated into this storyboard

Seeing Theory: directly changing a visible quantity gives the first interaction its teaching purpose. Here, the deposit boundary is the proposed control.

The Pudding: a focused visual carries the explanation. Here, the composition leaves the house to follow the monthly consequence, then returns to it.

The Evolution of Trust: experiments should produce understanding. The paired “try more / try less” actions test the competing opportunities.

Wise: the brief’s reference for spacious, confident hierarchy. The review uses warm paper, large figures and restrained forest/coral.

These are design interpretations, not replicated assets. Reference pages and the previous public review were inspected; no claim of a full usability study of those products is made. Previous experiments remain available at the original review URL.
