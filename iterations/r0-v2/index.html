  3. Does the final reorganisation feel like being handed the controls, with your choice intact?

Built: three bounded SVG studies, pause/replay/manual progression, a seekable timeline, responsive layouts, reduced-motion key states, and three fixed deposit fixtures plus a date selector in C.

Still open: the full intro, arbitrary deposit entry, actual financial results, novice comprehension, final calculator composition, 2D/3D priority, advanced controls and access flows. None is settled here. No backend integration, engine change or R1 implementation.

Object continuity & choreography

A: the house, deposit boundary and savings envelope persist through the allocation change. The interest bracket remains outside the house. The 80% line is attached to its purchase body. A separately labelled monthly-payment slip is introduced, then that exact SVG object becomes the budget’s payment segment. The mortgage principal is never represented as monthly income.

B: each strategy has exactly one investment balance. The opening contribution enters at purchase; later tabs arrive from the monthly budget. The container follows the date marker. Fill sizes are schematic and returns are deliberately excluded to isolate contribution timing. No totals or winner are shown.

C: the same house, savings, deposit choice and date persist. Only composition and control availability change. “Your starting allocation” remains labelled when the comparison date changes; no future balance is fabricated.

Alternative considered: unfolding the entire mortgage into a budget. Rejected because it could confuse debt principal with monthly flow. The labelled payment slip provides a clear bridge.

Illustrative assumptions & evidence limits

Fixture r0-v2-fiction-v1: unrestricted cash only, after buying costs. The A experiment explicitly compares £40,000.00 and £80,000.00 deposits. Their mortgage/retained pairs are £360,000.00/£60,000.00 and £320,000.00/£20,000.00. The 80% boundary is fictional, not a lender offer. No numerical rate, repayment, interest total or return is calculated.

B holds a £20,000.00 cash reserve in both strategies. Initial investment is £0.00 for the larger deposit and £40,000.00 for the smaller deposit. Monthly additions are conceptual successor behaviour, with the same monthly funding and investment destination assumed. Investments can lose value. This contribution-only drawing excludes returns and cannot rank strategies.

C’s middle fixture is £60,000.00 deposit, £340,000.00 mortgage, £40,000.00 retained. Dates are comparison horizons, not mortgage terms. Selectors choose fixed illustrations; they are not financial calculations.
